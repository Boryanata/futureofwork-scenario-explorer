# Source Corpus Review

## Overall Assessment

The current corpus is strong enough to support the core capstone argument, especially the critique of technological determinism and the use of AI exposure as a baseline rather than a forecast. After adding Denmark and US/NYC policy sources, the comparative case corpus is now in much better shape.

The corpus is strongest on:

- AI exposure measurement
- AI and labor-market risk
- macroeconomic/productivity arguments
- institutional mediation theory
- U.S. workforce development
- Singapore policy and workforce transformation

The corpus is still thinner on:

- organizational/human-AI work design
- collective bargaining and social dialogue beyond broad OECD and Denmark sources
- direct evidence connecting institutional variables to specific scenario outcomes

## Core Sources Already Present

### Theoretical Framing

- `Jungherr - How Artificial Intelligence Shapes Social Change - Working Paper.pdf`
- `ACEMOGLU-THE SIMPLE MACROECONOMICS OF AI.pdf`
- `Automation and Repression_Acemoglu.pdf`
- `Public Sector Institutional Reform.pdf`

These support the project's non-deterministic framing: AI does not directly determine outcomes; institutions, governance, incentives, and power shape how technological change unfolds.

### AI Exposure And Labor Baselines

- `The OECD Exposure Measure.pdf`
- `OCCUPATIONAL EXPOSURE TO ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE BY GEOGRAPHY AND EDUCATION.pdf`
- `Daniel-Rock_masterclass-GPTs-areGPTs.pdf`
- `what can machines learn_pandp.20181019.pdf`
- `AI exposure predicts unemployment risk.pdf`
- `Artificial-Intelligence-and-Jobs-Evidence-from-Online-Vacancies.pdf`

These support the exposure baseline and help distinguish exposure from realized employment outcomes.

### Labor Outcomes And Policy Context

- `OECD Employment Outlook 2026.pdf`
- `Reecent Policy Developments on AI in the Labor Market.pdf`
- `Global AI Jobs Barometer2025.pdf`
- `Workforce Development Policy in the US_Brookings.pdf`
- `Dispelling-Myths-of-AI-and-Efficiency-1.pdf`
- `Managing the Human Fallback.pdf`

These support outcome dimensions such as displacement risk, job transitions, productivity, wages, skill change, and workforce adaptation.

## Verdict

The corpus is relevant and usable. It is no longer too thin on comparative cases. The AI exposure side is strong, and the Denmark/Singapore/US-NYC case folders now provide a plausible foundation for institutional presets.

The next best step is not to collect many more general AI papers. Instead, add only a small number of targeted human-AI work design and collective bargaining/social dialogue sources, then shift into evidence coding.
